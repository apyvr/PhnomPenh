Our Story

Legacy.

Four decades. Three generations. One room on East Georgia Street. This is how a family kept its recipes, and itself, together across a journey no one planned.

Cambodia

A table set in Phnom Penh.

Long before Vancouver knew the name, our grandfather ran a restaurant in the city of Phnom Penh. It was a gathering place for neighbours, for weddings, for weekday lunches, built on recipes measured by hand and by memory. That kitchen was the family's first school.

The 1970s

Walking out through the jungle.

When the Khmer Rouge tore through Cambodia, the life our grandfather had built was lost. The family left on foot, walking for weeks through jungle and uncertainty, crossing into Vietnam with almost nothing. What survived the journey was the way a dish is remembered, the names of the children, and the decision to stay together.

December 1979

Arriving in Vancouver.

The family reached Canada in the cold of December 1979, part of the wave of refugees arriving on a new coast. An immigration officer saw a family rather than a file, and worked to bring the whole of it to these shores. That act of kindness is part of the story, and we tell it so it is not forgotten.

The Early Years

Saving toward a stove.

The first years in Vancouver were long. Our mother took in sewing as a seamstress. Our father worked in gardening and landscaping. Every shift and every saved dollar pointed the same direction. They started small with a noodle stall, weathered early setbacks with permits and paperwork, and refused to cut corners until the doors could open the right way.

1985

244 East Georgia Street.

In 1985 the restaurant opened at 244 East Georgia Street. Our father installed the wainscoting himself, panel by panel, and that same wood still lines the walls today. Run your hand along it and you are touching the year we began. The recipes had crossed a jungle and an ocean to arrive here, and they have not changed since.

A Family Choice

One room, one family.

Over the years there were offers to expand, to open a second location and a third. The family chose to stay a single Chinatown room. Not because growth was hard, but because the point was never the number of doors. It was being able to work side by side. To stay one kitchen was to stay one family.

Today

Three generations at the pass.

The restaurant is run by the second and third generations together. The founders' children and grandchildren work the kitchen and the front of house, measuring the same recipes the same way. The room has been recognized by the Michelin Guide every year since its Vancouver debut in 2022.

The room is the same one it has always been. Walk in and add your name to the list.
