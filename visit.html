Getting here

Parking, transit and the walk.

Parking

Metered street parking runs along East Georgia and the surrounding blocks. The Chinatown Plaza parkade at 180 Keefer Street is a short walk from the door.

Transit

Stadium Chinatown Station is about a ten minute walk. Several bus routes stop on Main Street, one block from the restaurant.

A note on the area

Chinatown borders the Downtown Eastside and some blocks feel rough around the edges. The street itself is busy through lunch and dinner. As anywhere downtown, keep valuables out of sight in a parked car.

Our block

200 East Georgia Street.

One block, a century deep

There is only one 200 block of East Georgia Street. It runs a single block between Main Street and Gore Avenue, and it holds more than a century of Vancouver history. Once known as Harris Street, it saw its first buildings in the late 1880s. By the early 1900s it was part of Chinatown's eastward growth, lined with homes, grocers, wholesalers and family businesses.

A working street

For generations, produce was unloaded here and families lived above their businesses. Grocers, printers, restaurants and merchants served a neighbourhood that kept changing while remaining unmistakably Chinatown. At 252 to 260 East Georgia, the Louie family built the grocery business a later generation would grow into IGA and London Drugs.

Old and new

That mixture is still what makes the block special. Old brick buildings sit beside newer ones, and traditional markets share the street with restaurants drawing people from across Vancouver. The city once described the block as an ecosystem of commercial, social and residential life bridging Chinatown's old and new.

Phnom Penh became part of that story in the 1980s, beginning with a small noodle operation across the street before finding its home here on East Georgia. So when you come to eat, look around. The block outside our doors is part of the restaurant's story, part of Chinatown's story, and part of a neighbourhood that has been feeding, housing and bringing people together for well over a century.

While you are here

Make an afternoon of it.

Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Garden

The first full scale classical Chinese garden built outside China, a short walk west on Carrall Street.

Chinatown Storytelling Centre

Exhibits on the families who built the neighbourhood, on East Pender Street a few blocks from the restaurant.

The surrounding blocks

Bakeries, barbecue shops, coffee and small bars line East Georgia and Main Street. The Millennium Gate stands over West Pender, about ten minutes away on foot.
